What, fundamentally, is an LLM? At the lowest level, it only predicts the conditional probability of the next token given the existing token sequence, and continuously produces text through this probabilistic generation process. Yet what we actually observe extends far beyond tokens: highly abstract structures such as mathematics, logic, programs, and causal relations can all appear stably on top of this generation mechanism.

A seemingly simple question then arises: if these highly abstract structures can all form from token probabilities, what about structures such as personality, values, behavior, and even social relations?

Are they merely another class of high-level structures produced by the same probabilistic generation space? If so, how should we understand such structures? More importantly, what is the actual relationship between these human structures produced by LLMs and the human structures in the real world?

## 1. A Unified Generation Mechanism

Any observable structure produced by an LLM is, in essence, a derived distribution formed after its token-sequence probability distribution undergoes generation, combination, conditioning, and observational mapping. These high-level structures are not another kind of thing that exists alongside the underlying probability distribution; rather, they are the statistical structures that the underlying probability distribution exhibits under specific conditions.

In fact, we have already accepted a rather strong fact: **a model that only predicts conditional probabilities at the token level can stably produce highly abstract structures at higher levels—mathematics, logic, programs, causal relations, and so on.** These structures are clearly not token-level variables, but we do not therefore regard them as impossible. After large-scale training, these high-level structures have already become basic capabilities of LLMs.

Then, when we face personality, behavior, values, and social relations, why do we feel the need to single out a special question of "whether LLMs can produce these structures"?

If highly abstract structures such as mathematics and logic can stably exist as derived structures of token probabilities, then personality, behavior, and even social structures, as other derived structures in the same probability space, are in principle nothing special. They certainly differ in content, organization, and complexity, but from the perspective of the generative essence of LLMs, they are all, first and foremost, high-level structures that recur in language and experience, possess internal relations, and exhibit statistical regularities.

Therefore, what truly needs to be explained is no longer "whether high-level structures can emerge from token probability." This point has already been fully demonstrated by LLM capabilities in mathematics, logic, and programming. That high-level structures can form from token probabilities should be regarded as a general property of LLMs, rather than a special hypothesis that must be re-proposed for each kind of structure.

This does not mean that any structure will automatically appear, nor that the structures produced by LLMs necessarily reproduce the real world accurately. "Being able to derive a certain structure" and "the derived structure being consistent with real-world structures" are two different questions. The former concerns the possibility of generation; the latter concerns the content and properties of the structure, and its correspondence with the real world.

Accordingly, the research question should undergo an important shift:

Not: Can LLMs produce personality, behavior, and social structures from next-token prediction?

But rather: What kinds of personality, behavior, and social structures do LLMs actually produce? To what extent do these structures correspond to human structures in reality? Under what conditions can this correspondence emerge stably?

This shift is important. Otherwise, we easily fall into an unnecessary dualism: mathematics and logic can emerge from token probability, and we accept this fact; yet personality, behavior, and social structures, which come from the same probabilistic generation mechanism, are presupposed as special objects whose "possibility" requires additional proof.

In fact, there is no need to presuppose personality, behavior, and social networks as particularly mysterious structures within LLMs. Their real difference from mathematics and logic lies not in that one can be produced by token probability while the other cannot, but in that **they belong to different types of high-level structures, and therefore require different empirical methods to identify and validate.** Mathematics can be validated through the correctness of theorems, programs through the results of execution, while personality, behavior, and social structures need to be validated through corresponding human empirical data, statistical distributions, and structural relations.

Thus, the next-token probability of LLMs provides us with a unified generative essence, while empirical research on different high-level structures answers what these probabilities actually form after different conditions and observational mappings. We no longer need to prove that personality, as a high-level structure, "can exist" in the probability space of LLMs; what is truly worth studying is whether it forms something there that has stable structure, measurable features, and can establish correspondence with real human structures.

## 2. Reduction to Text

On closer thought, these capabilities that we are already very familiar with are, at the level of the LLM's generation mechanism, not fundamentally different from measurements of human personality and behavioral traits. They can all ultimately be reduced to problems of textual probability distributions. For an LLM, they are, in essence, the same thing: high-level structures derived from token conditional-probability distributions that can be observed and measured by us.

For example, an agent "clicking a button" appears on the surface to be a non-linguistic behavior, but if this agent is driven by an LLM, then in the actual generation process, which button to choose still needs to be realized through some textual decision, tool call, or action expression. The same holds for personality questionnaires: so-called "choosing option 3" ultimately still requires the model to generate an output corresponding to that choice. At the experimental level, we can interpret these results separately as "behavioral events" and "personality measurement results," but from the perspective of the LLM's generative essence, they can all be reduced to probabilistic events over text sequences.

Therefore, mathematical reasoning, logical reasoning, programming, personality-questionnaire choices, and behavioral decisions do not exhibit a fundamental categorical break in the generative essence of LLMs. They can all be abstracted into the same process:

$$ \text{token conditional probability} \rightarrow \text{text generation} \rightarrow \text{observed variables} $$

The only difference lies in the different observation and measurement methods we apply to the generated results.

The reason we today believe that LLMs possess logical, mathematical, and programming capabilities is also not because we found an independently existing "logic module" or "math module" inside the model, but because we observe that a large number of generated results possess stable logical relations, mathematical structures, and program structures. In other words, we have in fact already accepted a very important fact: highly abstract structures far removed from the token level can stably exist as derived structures of token probability distributions.

Then why should personality and behavior be regarded as a completely different problem?

If "choosing A" in a personality questionnaire is ultimately also a textual event generated by the LLM, then it is not fundamentally different, in the generation mechanism, from the model generating a mathematical proof, a program, or an answer satisfying logical relations. We have merely applied personality, mathematical, or logical measurements to these generated results respectively. Therefore, starting from the already-verified capabilities of LLMs, a reasonable prior assumption should be: there likewise exists the ability to derive personality, behavior, and other human high-level structures from token probabilities.

Of course, this is not to say that we already know these derived structures are human structures, nor that any high-level structure will automatically and accurately reproduce the real world. What is truly unknown is the concrete form of these structures, and the exact correspondence between them and real human structures. But "whether LLMs can produce high-level structures such as personality and behavior" need no longer be treated as a particularly mysterious question. Since structures such as mathematics, logic, and programming can all be stably produced from the same probabilistic generation mechanism, personality and behavior, as other observed structures under the same generation mechanism, face no special obstacle in principle.

And once we further observe the ability to measure personality traits, this prior is directly reinforced. Logic, mathematics, and programming first tell us that token probabilities can derive highly abstract and stable structures; if we further find that, under given persona conditions, an LLM can stably produce personality traits corresponding to that persona, and that different personas form distinguishable statistical distributions, then what we obtain is no longer merely general evidence of "the ability to generate high-level structures," but a more direct observation that human structures tied to specific human individual conditions can be derived from this probability space.

Thus, the research question undergoes a fundamental shift: we no longer primarily ask "whether LLMs can produce personality, behavior, and social structures from text generation," but rather should ask:

What, exactly, are the personality, behavior, and social structures it produces from text probability? To what extent are they consistent with the corresponding structures in the human world? What conditions enable this correspondence to emerge stably?

In this way, the entire research logic becomes coherent: we already know that token probability can derive high-level structures such as logic, mathematics, and programming; therefore, deriving other high-level structures should be regarded as a general capability of LLMs; personality and behavior are no exception in their generative essence; observing personality structures under persona conditions further strengthens this expectation; and the truly scientifically significant question turns to the correspondence between these derived structures and real human structures.

This also means that social-simulation research need no longer start from the question of "how can language models possibly produce social structures." We already know, through extensive practice, that the same generation mechanism can produce complex structures far beyond the token level. What is truly worth studying is: when this generation mechanism confronts the human world, what exactly does it recover?

## 3. From a Common Origin to Different High-Level Structures

Although for LLMs all generation is ultimately built on the conditional probability distribution over tokens, this does not mean that the high-level structures thus formed are identical in nature. Logic, mathematics, and programming are a class of highly formalized structures with clear rules, symbol systems, and verifiable standards of correctness; personality, values, and behavioral tendencies belong to another class—statistical structures concerning people's psychological states, preferences, decisions, and action patterns. Therefore, from a "common generative origin" one cannot directly infer that "different high-level structures have the same nature"; research on personality, values, and behavior must still rely on independent empirical testing.

But there is a very important distinction here: although personality, values, and behavior differ in their concrete forms of expression, they lie within the same, more specific space of human structures. They are not three isolated problems, but different manifestations that are mutually related and mutually constrained. Personality describes relatively stable individual tendencies, values embody more specific preferences and normative orientations, and behavior is the external expression of these tendencies in concrete situations. Hence, if we can reliably observe that one of these structures is reproduced in LLMs, we will not merely have obtained isolated evidence about that one structure, but will at the same time provide a certain degree of support for other phenomena in the same structural domain.

### The Special Value of Personality Research

This makes personality a very valuable entry point. Personality itself is already a low-dimensional structure that results from a high degree of summarization over a vast amount of human behavioral differences. It is naturally connected to values, preferences, decision-making styles, and behavioral tendencies, so personality is not an abstract psychometric variable unrelated to real behavior. On the contrary, it can be regarded as a structural interface connecting "internal characteristics of a person" and "external behavior of a person."

Therefore, if experiments find that a persona-conditioned LLM can not only produce stable and distinguishable personality traits, but that these traits systematically approach the distribution of human personality as persona information becomes richer, then the significance of this result is not limited to "LLMs can complete personality questionnaires." It first shows that the generation space of LLMs genuinely contains human personality structures that can be activated and modulated by personas; furthermore, because personality is structurally connected to values, preferences, and behavior, this finding also provides an empirically supported entry point for studying these adjacent human attributes.

The key point here is not "personality is proven, so values and behavior automatically follow." More precisely, there exists an empirical mutual-corroboration relationship between human structures at different levels. If personality, values, and behavior were completely independent of one another, then each would have to be verified from scratch; but if they are, in the first place, mutually related structures within the same space of human states, then a successful reproduction of one of them will change our prior judgment about whether the other structures can be reproduced by the same generation mechanism.

### The Structural Convenience This Brings to Empirical Research

This actually brings an important experimental convenience to LLM-based human simulation research: there is no need to decompose "human structures" into countless mutually independent capabilities and prove each one separately.

Suppose we study a social-simulation system, and what we ultimately hope to validate is a series of structures—personality, values, preferences, decisions, behavior, and even social relations. If every variable were regarded as a completely independent problem, the cost of validation would rapidly balloon. But real human structures are not organized in this way. Personality influences value orientations, values influence choices, choices form behavioral patterns, and behavior in turn forms social relations and event histories. Although these structures can be distinguished in statistical measurement, they are extensively correlated in their generation mechanisms and empirical data.

Therefore, it is possible to form an empirical path that extends from local structures to adjacent structures: first find a variable that has a mature measurement system, abundant human benchmark data, and structural connections to other human attributes—for example, personality; prove that LLMs can stably produce this structure under persona conditions, and that its distribution can approach the human distribution; then study values, preferences, decisions, and behavior. If these adjacent structures also exhibit conditional dependencies and human-distribution features consistent with personality, then the evidence is not simply additive, but rather a cross-validation of the same larger hypothesis space under different observation windows.

This also means that research on LLM generation of human structures cannot remain at the single question of "whether LLMs can simulate personality." Logic, mathematics, and programming have already shown that token probabilities can form complex and stable high-level structures; personality experiments further show that this capacity for high-level-structure formation can enter the concrete space of human structures. And since personality, values, and behavior are themselves tightly connected structurally, personality can become an important fulcrum for further research on human-behavior generation.

Therefore, the significance of a single personality experiment is not simply to prove that LLMs can already simulate humans, but to provide a structural empirical starting point. On top of the common origin of token probabilities, if we have already observed a high-level structure with a clear correspondence to humans, then this result simultaneously provides an empirical basis for studying other adjacent human structures. Because personality is intrinsically connected to values, preferences, decisions, and behavior, subsequent research on these structures no longer starts entirely from scratch, but can build on the empirical results at the personality level, gradually extending to more complex human structures.

This is also the true meaning of the so-called structural convenience. It does not reduce the empirical work required by subsequent research, but makes the empirical studies at different levels and of different types transferable to one another. Successful observation of personality structures can become a structural fulcrum for studying values, behavior, and social structures; and once these adjacent structures are further validated, they can in turn reinforce the recognition that a wider range of human structures can be derived from the same probabilistic generation mechanism.

Therefore, the value of personality research lies not only in whether it can itself be accurately simulated, but also in that it occupies a connecting position among multiple high-level human structures. It is at once a structure that can be independently measured and validated, and empirically related to other structures such as values and behavior. It is precisely for this reason that empirical results at the personality level possess research value that extends beyond the single task of personality itself.
